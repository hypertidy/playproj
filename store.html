Drag turns the view: the globe about its north, a map like a turntable, about its upright sideways and over its edge and under it upwards. Shift-drag or right-drag moves it; the wheel zooms toward the pointer; double-click a place to turn about it, or Shift-double-click to look through the surface and turn about what hangs below it: a curtain, a hung profile, the sea floor.

Everything kept per profile

How it works

A gridded variable is an array with a CRS: the numbers along its last two dimensions are columns and rows of cells that the store places in some coordinate system, with an affine transform or with 1D coordinates. This page finds that CRS, draws the grid's cells and chunks on the globe, and unfolds them into another projection.

Cells that are equal in the store are rarely equal on the earth. Colour by cell area to see by how much, or by angular distortion to see where the store's CRS bends shapes.

A grid with depth has a column under every cell. A chunk usually holds many levels (25 in the ACCESS-ESM1.6 preset), so a click reads the cell's whole column for a chunk or two, often one the map has already. Under it, the model's floor and GEBCO 2026's: the model's is somewhere in its deepest level with a value (partial cells), GEBCO's is averaged over the cell, and the readout says whether GEBCO falls above, within or below that level. Or map the bottom: each cell's deepest level with a value, for twice a level's reads, which shows where the bottom waters reach across the basins. Its colours are fitted to bottoms deeper than 1,000 m, so the shelves run off the top.

A section cuts through the picked cell along its row or its column: every level of every cell along it, a few chunks, drawn true to depth with the model's rock below and GEBCO's floor as a sand line. Under the map it hangs as a curtain, sunk as the profiles are; the map goes see-through, so tilt the view to see it. It takes the map's colours: map a deep level, or the bottom, to bring out the deep water masses, the surface to see the whole column.

A collection of profiles (Argo floats, CTD tags on seals: one latitude and longitude per profile, not per cell) is no grid, so it is drawn as a point per profile. A click reads that profile alone, a single chunk where the store keeps one chunk per profile, and plots it.

Time and depth come together through a window. The strip counts profiles over time; a window of days picks the profiles taken in it, and Play steps it on, skipping spans with none. Colour by the data, or hang the profiles, and the page reads every profile in the window, all of its levels at once. A virtual store's refs to neighbouring profiles in one file are read together, so a tag's month of profiles is a few requests, not hundreds. The byte ranges a virtual store's chunks point at are held in this browser once read, as a table of path, offset, size and the version of the file the store recorded, with the bytes beside it, so a step of a slider that has been seen before, on this visit or an earlier one, asks the source for nothing; an Icechunk store's manifests and snapshots, which never change under their names, are held the same way. So are the ranges read of the sea floor's file (GEBCO), under the version the file itself gives. Once a slice is in, the page fetches the bytes of the next step the way the slider last moved, and decodes them only when the slider gets there. Then depth is a place, not a read: moving the depth recolours the points with each profile's value there, and hung profiles go down from their points, sunk by depth times the exaggeration, with a bead where each crosses that depth. The colours span every level of the window's profiles, so they hold still as the depth moves; fit them to the depth and they span only the values there, refitted as it moves, which brings out the small differences at depth that the full range washes out. A map of one depth across every profile would still read every chunk.

Under them, the sea floor: GEBCO 2026 read over the window's box from the overview that suits it (and at its full 15 arc seconds under a picked profile), sunk by the same exaggeration, and drawn along a section; in 3D it is a swath along the window's tracks. Colour by height above the sea floor to see which profiles reach the bottom, and which go deeper than GEBCO has it: a seal's dive is a sounding.

A chunk can be a whole GRIB message, as in dynamical.org's virtual stores of ECMWF and NOAA forecasts: the store points at byte ranges in the agencies' own files, and gribberish, compiled to WebAssembly, decodes each message here when it is read.
