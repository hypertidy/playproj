How it works

Draw equal small circles all over the globe, then project them. Each lands on the map as (very nearly) an ellipse, and that ellipse is the projection's whole story at that point: its long and short axes a and b are the largest and smallest scale there. If every ellipse is a circle the map is conformal, and shapes are right in the small. If every ellipse has the same area (ab = 1) the map is equal-area. No map can be both.

Nicolas Auguste Tissot worked this out in 1859. The ellipse is the image of a circle under the projection's local linear map, its Jacobian. "Indicatrix" draws that ellipse; "Projected circles" projects the actual circle point by point, and the two part company as the circles grow. The red and blue lines are where the parallel and the meridian through the point go, with scales k and h; they cross at right angles only where θ′ is 90°.

Every ellipse is drawn at the same ground radius, so its size on the map is the scale there. "Fit the spacing" picks that radius so the ellipses fill a share of the gap to their neighbours, the way you would choose scale by eye in the R tissot package; "fixed radius" sets it yourself, in degrees of arc. Centres can sit on the graticule or on a square grid laid over the map, which suits a regional projection. "Shape only" redraws every ellipse with the same area, so what is left is how much it is stretched and which way.

The indicatrix samples the distortion at a few points; the same factors can be worked out at every pixel. "Distortion field" picks one (areal scale, angular distortion, either axis, the scales along the meridian and parallel, the flattening a/b, how far the meridian and parallel are from crossing square, or the convergence) and shows it as an image, as contours at round values (the isolines of Snyder's Album of Map Projections), or as the colour of each ellipse. "Factors GeoTIFF" saves every factor PROJ reports over the map in view as float bands in the map's own CRS, named as proj_factors() names them.

The numbers are the ones PROJ reports from proj_factors(), worked out here from any forward projection by differencing it at the same four points around the picked one, with the same formulas (Snyder 1987); they match PROJ to about 1e-8. The calculation follows Bill Huber's answer on GIS Stack Exchange. The map is drawn by proj4js. The interface takes its lead from the tissot R package, which computes these factors through PROJ.
