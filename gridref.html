How it works

Every number here is computed for the pin, so a click on the map (or a at=lon,lat in the hash) tells the same story for somewhere else. The map is drawn in the pin's own UTM zone: its eastings and northings are the canvas, so the grid lines are straight and the meridians, parallels and coastlines bend. OpenStreetMap tiles are warped into the grid a few triangles at a time; the coastlines are Natural Earth's, projected point by point.

The projection is the transverse Mercator in Krueger's series to sixth order (Karney 2011), the method PROJ's tmerc uses, on WGS84 (GRS80 for MGA, a tenth of a millimetre away here). The point scale and grid convergence are differenced along the meridian; test/check-gridref.mjs compares them, the eastings and northings and each zone's false origin with PROJ (test/make-gridref-fixture.py). The street directory is made up: its pages are 4 km by 5 km cut from the kilometre grid in each 100 km square, numbered across then down, with 500 m squares lettered A to H and numbered 1 to 10. Real directories each have their own scheme. In Tasmania the page uses the real books instead: the street atlas and map book pages theLIST publishes, looked up after taking the pin from GDA2020 to GDA94 (src/core/mapref.js, checked in test/check-mapref.mjs), and a second part of the story explains them.

Hash keys: s (step, from 0), at (lon,lat of the pin).
