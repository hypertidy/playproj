    How it works

    Australia's land runs from the Cocos (Keeling) Islands at 96.8 E to Norfolk Island at 168 E, and from Ashmore Reef at 12 S to Heard Island at 53 S. With the Australian Antarctic Territory it runs to the South Pole. An inset map puts the far pieces in boxes on the main map. This page does it the other way round. Every piece or group gets a panel of its own, a full map in its own CRS fitted to its land. One overview panel holds the true geography and the footprint of every other panel, numbered. The locator becomes the main thing, and no panel is a guest on another.

    Panels use GDA2020 where it has a grid: the Australian Albers (EPSG:9473) for the mainland and the MGA zone of each territory. The AAT uses the Antarctic polar stereographic (EPSG:3031). Grouped panels use a Lambert azimuthal on the middle of their group. The overview, and the one frame for everything, use a Lambert azimuthal on the middle of the land shown. The table gives what one frame for the same land would be, its size and the share of it that is land. For each panel it gives the width it shows, its scale against the first panel and the share of it that is land.

    The AAT is Antarctica between 45 E and 160 E less Adelie Land (136 11' E to 142 02' E), south of 60 S, as the Australian Antarctic Territory Act 1954 has it. Lord Howe Island is part of New South Wales and Macquarie Island part of Tasmania. The others are external territories. src/core/facets.js has the pieces, groups, panels, layout and footprints, checked in test/check-facets.mjs. Projections are those of src/core/inset.js and src/core/polarstere.js.

    Hash keys: s (step), on (pieces shown), split, shared, single, ctx, grat.
